Two proposed Story Block features: mockups

These are pictures of the real app (Story Block 2.6.0, Story Director 2.2.0, the Ersveil pack, a short scripted story) with the new bits drawn on top. Nothing here works and nothing in the code was changed.

Everything drawn reuses the app's own buttons, inputs, chips and cards, at the normal size. Desktop is 1280 x 900, phone is 390 x 844 (the two form pictures are taller). Click a picture to see it full size.

1. Edit what's already there

Every value the story keeps (items, money, place, weather, time, standing) gets a small pencil. Tap it and that one value turns into a small form on the spot. Nothing else on the screen moves. The change is saved with the latest reply, so a swipe or delete of that reply takes it back.

Inventory: a pencil beside each name

Inventory. Each item has a small pencil next to its name, the purse has one too, and an Add item button sits at the top of the list.

Inventory: one row being edited

One row being edited. Name, count and note become fields right in the row, with Save, Cancel and a Remove button. Torch is being changed from 2 to 3.

Add item

Add item. The same inline form, empty. It opens at the top of the list.

Purse and Where, with pencils

Purse and Where on Home. The wallet line (3 silver 8 copper) has a pencil, and so do the place, the time and the weather. (This one picture also covers the “pencils on place, weather and time” part of shot 3.)

Purse being edited

Purse being edited. One number field per coin (gold, silver, copper), a line that says what the total comes to (5 silver is 50 copper, 38 now), then Save and Cancel.

Place and weather being edited

Place and weather being edited. Place is plain text with a short list of suggestions from the map (any text still works). Weather is plain text. The time keeps its pencil.

Standing, with pencils

Ersveil Standing. Each power's number has a pencil. (Standing lives in the Story panel, under More, because the pack does not put it in the Game view.)

Standing being edited

One standing being edited. A number field that only takes whole numbers from -100 to 100 (the pack's own range), with Save and Cancel. Adventurer Guild goes from 2 to 5.

Changed this reply, after edits

The “Changed this reply” card after edits. The story's own lines first, then one line per thing you set, and a small hint that your edits ride on this reply: a swipe or delete takes them back. (In this mock the lines run the full width of the card so each one reads in full.)

2. Make your own block

No pack needed. You make a small sheet in the chat (a name and a list of fields), the story is told what is in it and can change it, and you can change it too. I put it in a new Blocks tab of the Game view: the form and the cards need more room than a Home tile gives, and Home stays as it is. On a phone the new tab sits under More.

Blocks tab, nothing yet

The Blocks tab, before any block exists. A short explanation and a + New block button.

New block form

The new block form. A block name, a note for the story, then a list of fields. Each field has a name and a type: Number (with lowest and highest), Text, Choice (with its options) or List. + Add field adds another; Save block finishes. (Tall pictures: the screen scrolls on a real device.)

The finished block

The finished block as a card. Mood “furious at Ren” (written by the story), Fatigue 6/10 as a gauge, Relationship: Ren is warm, Known contacts are Mara (quartermaster) and Old Teo. Every value has a pencil. The footer says the story can update these and that you can tap the pencil to change them.

The block's menu

The card's “…” menu. Edit fields (back to the form) and Export as pack (turn the block into a pack file).

What the story changed

The story's side. When a reply moves a block, the “Changed this reply” card has one line for it: Wessel's Notes: Mood → 'furious at Ren'; Fatigue 4 → 6.

3. Blocks with their own tab and functions

A bigger step for the custom blocks: a block can have its own tab in the Game view, and it can carry buttons, rules and timers that change its values.

A block as its own tab

A block can be its own tab. Notes and Skills sit in the Game view tab bar next to Director. The Notes page shows Wessel's Notes full width with its values (each with a pencil) and, at the bottom, its buttons: Rest and Steady breath. On a phone the new tabs fold under More (second picture, More open); the third picture is the Notes page on a phone.

The block editor, extended

The block editor now has two more parts. Show as picks Own tab, Card on Home, or Both, and for an own tab you give the tab a name and choose an icon. Functions has + Add function, which offers Button, Rule or Timer. One of each is filled in: the button Rest (Fatigue minus 3, not below 0; Mood set to “rested”), a rule (when Fatigue is at least 10, Mood becomes “exhausted”), and a timer (every 8 story hours, Fatigue plus 1). Each type has a one-line hint underneath. The fields are folded to one line each here to keep the picture shorter. (Tall pictures: the screen scrolls on a real device.)

Templates

Save as template. The block's “…” menu now lists Edit fields, Save as template and Export as pack.

New block chooser

+ New block opens a small chooser: Start empty, or From a template, with the two saved templates (Wessel's Notes, Survival).

After pressing Rest

After pressing Rest. The reply card says what you pressed and what moved: You pressed Rest (Wessel's Notes): Fatigue 6 → 3; Mood → 'rested', with a hint that the press rides on the reply and a swipe or delete takes it back.

When a rule fires

When a rule fires (a second example). The story moves Fatigue to 10, and the card adds a line for the rule: Rule: Fatigue reached 10 → Mood 'exhausted'.
