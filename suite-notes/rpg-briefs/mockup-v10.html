Lean RPG: stats, stage bars and automatic rolls

Taken in the real app: Story Block 2.7.2 with the Ersveil chat. The bars, steppers and radar are Story Block's own widgets; the rest was placed for the shots and does nothing yet. Changed from v1 after your notes: there's no Roll button (rolls are automatic), Health/Mana/Stamina are coloured stage bars, and class and rank rise on their own.

0 · The Party tab: one card per character

One Party tab holds a card for you and each companion: name and role, Soul Class, an XP bar, the three stage bars, the seven stats in a row, and their skills with ranks. A card with unspent points shows "N points to spend" and a Spend points button; the others have Stats. Either opens the stat distribution below (steppers and radar) for that character. Skills opens their skills list.

1 · Spend points / Stats (opened from a card)

Health, Mana and Stamina are bars in one aligned row. Each bar is split into its stages, and its colour shows how bad it is: green near full, amber in the middle, red near empty. The word sits at the right end ("Hurt", "Fresh", "Spent"); the story moves them and you can tap one to fix it. Below them is RB's Stats step: steppers spending the shared points, and the radar with the new shape over the old one.

2 · Automatic rolls and level-ups

You write what you do, as usual. Before the reply, a small AI call reads it and picks who acts, with which skill and stat, against whom; code rolls the dice; the narrator tells the result, and the black battle log (approved version D) shows it in the message. There's no button to press. A swipe rolls again. Settings → Lean RPG has "Rolls: automatic / off".

Soul Class is a tier, so it rises by itself: at 100 XP it goes up one and XP goes back to 0. Skill ranks do the same at 100 practice. Both show as lines in "Changed this reply", with no card to confirm, and a swipe takes them back like anything else in the reply.

3 · Skills (opened from a card) new in v4

Skills on a Party card opens that character's skills in place of the cards, like Spend points. Each skill shows its rank, the stat it rolls with and what it uses (Stamina or Mana), what it does (written at creation; the AI reads it), a mastery bar and its full roll bonus. The mastery bar has four cells, one per rank, coloured like the rank: Novice grey, Adept blue, Expert purple, Master green; the next cell fills with practice. "Rolls with it: d20 +12 = VIT 12 → +6 · Expert +2 · Soul Class II +4" shows exactly what the dice add. An empty slot says the story fills it when the character learns something new. Rank and practice only move through play (a swipe takes them back), so the pencil fixes only the name and what it does. Back to the party returns to the cards.

These shots are from the build in progress, so the tab bar already shows one Party tab without a tab for each Character or Skills block.

4 · New character new in v5

A wizard in Story Block's own dialog, laid out like the creation wizard you approved before: the summary row on top, cards in a grid. It opens from + New character on the Party tab, or from "Create your character" when the party is empty. Four steps:

  • Who: the name (from your persona), you or a companion, and Who they are: a short description, up to 240 characters, that the AI reads every reply.
  • Stats: the Spend points view from section 1, with the starting points.
  • Skills: start from a template, change anything, or write your own. Up to 5 skills, each with What it does (up to 120 characters, the AI reads it), a Stat dropdown (STR to CHA: what the roll adds), a Uses dropdown (Stamina or Mana: an Empty bar blocks the skill) and a Starts at dropdown: Novice, or Adept for one skill only. Nothing about rank or practice can be typed in. Templates are built in (Guild fighter, River scout, Hedge mage, Field medic, Silver tongue) and a pack can add its own; the Sealwarden recruit card shows one from Ersveil.
  • Review: everything on one page, then Create, which makes the Character and Skills blocks in one go.

Shot over an empty chat, as at the start of a story. The dialog is the real one; on your screen the chat behind it is dimmed and blurred.

5 · Combat new in v7

There is no separate combat mode: you write what you do, the dice are rolled before the reply, and the narrator's reply carries the battle log (display D, approved earlier) where it tells the fight. These shots are the real battle log from the build, filled with a made-up two-round fight.

Two fixes I propose (shown below): (1) a foe's attack reads from the attacker's side, Hit (red), Grazed (amber) or Blocked (green), with the attacker's total first; as built, "attacks Wessel — Success" meant Wessel blocked, which reads the wrong way round. (2) The Status bars are coloured by stage, green to amber to red, like the Party tab, instead of red for every health bar.

A Fight tab in place of the plain Foes block tab, shown while any foe slot is filled: the place and exchange, each foe with its class and a health stage bar (a downed foe greyed, a pencil to fix one), the party's three bars, and the last exchange's battle log with See it in the story. Nothing to press to fight: the note at the top says rolls are automatic.

6 · Settings → Lean RPG: your own dice and prompts new in v8

A page of its own in Story Suite settings, under Features:

  • Rolls: Automatic or Off (as before).
  • Difficulty: Gentle, Standard (today's numbers) or Harsh, or Custom with the six numbers behind every roll: the foe's base, what Soul Class adds, how much of the stat counts, how far short still counts as "at a cost", what makes an exchange decisive, and the most exchanges in one reply. A bar shows the odds for one of your characters against an even foe and updates as you change them (Wessel: Standard 61 / 8 / 31, Gentle 69 / 10 / 22, Harsh 52 / 5 / 43). New numbers count from the next reply; dice already rolled keep the numbers they were rolled with, so a Re-read or a reload never changes an old fight.
  • What the narrator is told about combat: the Combat rules block's text, editable here (up to 1,200 characters) with Reset to the default. It belongs to the chat, so each story can play differently.
  • Roll planner: your own instructions (up to 400 characters) added to the short AI call that plans the dice, e.g. "companions play it safe". Its full prompt can be read in a fold, but its answer format stays fixed so the dice can always be read.

7 · Starting a new chat new in v9

When the RPG is on and the story has no character yet, Home shows a Your character card at the top. Create your character opens the New character wizard; Not now hides the card for this chat (the Party tab's "Create your character" stays).

The wizard's Who step now fills Who they are from your active persona's description too (the first 240 characters), as it already fills the name. A note under the box says where it came from; you can change it.

7b · The first send without a character new in v10

If the RPG is on and you press Send before you have a character, the message is held (never lost) and this dialog opens. Create your character, then send opens the wizard and sends the held message when you press Create. Send without the RPG for now sends it as usual, with no dice this reply. Closing the dialog puts the text back in the message box. It asks once per chat; after "without", only the Home card from section 7 reminds you.

8 · Companions who join proposal, v9

When someone joins the party in the story, the RPG can make their character for you. Only party members get a character sheet; other people in the story stay ordinary characters (NPC State Delta keeps their dossiers).

  • The narrator writes one line when someone joins: + party Mara | about: a hedge-witch from Kvildal | class: I | best: int. Code makes the Character block (and an empty Skills block): stats 10, the named best stat 14, Soul Class as written (I to IX, never above the story's), bars full.
  • Ask (the default): the reply gets a card "Mara joins the party: Add to party · Edit first · No". Edit first opens the wizard already filled in.
  • Automatically: the character is made at once and shown in "Changed this reply" (a swipe takes it back).
  • Off: no line is offered; you make companions yourself.
  • A setting in Settings → RPG picks Ask, Automatically or Off. The usual limits still hold (at most 8 characters; room in the story's actions).
  • Someone who leaves: - party Mara moves them out of the party (kept, greyed, "Left") rather than deleting them.

9 · Blocks move to Story Suite settings new in v10

The Game view's generic Blocks tab goes. Making and managing blocks moves to Story Suite settings → Blocks: New block (empty) or From a template, then for each block Show as its own tab, a card on Home, both, or hidden, with Edit (fields, buttons, rules, tab name and icon) and ⋯ (save as a template, remove). RPG blocks are listed too, but shown by the RPG's Party and Fight tabs.

How a skill's roll is defined

Each skill names one stat and one resource when it is made (from a template or by you). A roll with it is d20 + half that stat (rounded down) + the rank (Novice 0, Adept 1, Expert 2, Master 3) + 2 × Soul Class, plus an edge of −3 to +3 for gear or position, against the foe's d20 + 5 + 2 × its class. The AI that plans the rolls no longer picks a stat for a skilled action: the skill's own stat is used. It still picks a stat for actions with no skill (climbing with no climbing skill uses the stat it judges right, at rank 0).
