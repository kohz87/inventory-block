A · closest to today

Quiet Glass

Today's glass panels, tidied: one floating header with tabs as a pill, soft tiles, one accent. The least to relearn.

Hanken Grotesk

Feels like: today, with the clutter taken out.

Best for: keeping what you know; smallest build.

Trade-off: the least distinctive of the three.

B · reads like a journal

Chronicle

A quiet side rail and a page that reads like the story's own record: a book serif for what happened, plain sans for the facts. Changes sit in the margin of each reply.

Newsreader + Instrument Sans

Feels like: a well-kept campaign journal.

Best for: slow, character-driven play; reading on a phone.

Trade-off: numbers are quieter; fights and stats get less space.

C · plays like a game

Field HUD

A compact heads-up bar and a grid of panels with hard edges and condensed labels. Everything at a glance, one amber accent for what needs you.

Barlow Condensed + Barlow

Feels like: a tactics game's field screen.

Best for: fights, travel and the RPG; lots of numbers at once.

Trade-off: busier; less room for the story's own words.

My recommendation

  • Chronicle (B) as the base, because the suite is there to serve the story: the dateline, the journal page and changes as margin notes keep the tracking quiet.
  • Borrow from Field HUD (C) for the Party and Fight screens, where numbers matter: the stat row, the stage bars and the amber deadlines.
  • Ship the shared structure first (6 tabs, one readout, one card per reply, presets) in today's look, then restyle. That way the simplification lands sooner and the look can change without reworking it.

Reply with A, B, C or a mix. The chosen direction then gets a full mockup in the real app (every tab, desktop and phone) before any build.
