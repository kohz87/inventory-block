Redesign · proposal 4 · 1 Nov

Three game layouts

Not three colours this time: three different ways to lay out a game screen. Each one draws its desktop and phone from the same blocks. Switch layouts and the theme; all three use the same Ersveil content.

1 · Hero sheetLike an RPG character screen: your character is the centrepiece (portrait, level badge, Health/Mana/Stamina rings, XP, stats), with the quest tracker, party and an item grid beside it.
Feels likeA character menu in an action RPG.
Strong atFights, levelling, gear and the party: the numbers you care about are big.
Weaker atTravel and the world: those live one tab away.
2 · JourneyThe world is the screen: the map fills the page, with your status, resources and a quest tracker floating over it like a game HUD, and the road ahead as a timeline along the bottom.
Feels likeAn open-world game's field view with a quest tracker.
Strong atTravel, what's moving, deadlines on the road; the timeline from Ersveil's real routes.
Weaker atDetailed stats: the character is a compact card.
3 · Quest boardLike a mobile RPG lobby: a resource bar on top (coin, time, weather, place), then shelves of cards: your party as character cards, quests as notice cards with rewards and deadlines, and the bag as item slots.
Feels likeA gacha or mobile RPG home screen.
Strong atSeeing everything at once as collectible cards; very touch-friendly.
Weaker atLong text: descriptions open on tap.
